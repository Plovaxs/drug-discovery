@ch X | CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

@sec Conclusions

The thesis asked whether learned guidance signals improve the molecules of a frozen, pocket-conditioned diffusion model on real outcomes, that is, on quantities that are computed independently of the guidance model. The question was tested with a frozen TargetDiff generator, position-only guidance on the clean-data estimate, two families of affinity predictors and one synthesizability predictor, and an evaluation harness consisting of AutoDock Vina docking, PoseBusters validity, ligand efficiency and the real RAscore, with corrected statistics, bootstrap intervals and negative controls. The following conclusions are drawn.

(1) *No route produced a defensible improvement.* Gradient guidance with an EGNN affinity predictor, with a physics-anchored GIGN + PIGNet2 predictor and with three variants of the mechanism (gradient normalisation, classifier reformulation, timestep windowing), rejection sampling with the EGNN as a ranker, gradient guidance toward synthesizability, and a redesigned training target (delta-learning against the docking score) each failed their pre-declared criteria. None of the 56 pocket-level gradient comparisons of Tracks A and B (9 and 24 in the two tiers of Track A, 23 in Track B) was significant after correction within its checkpoint. The failures were not attributable to low predictor accuracy: the predictors reached Pearson correlations of 0.58 to 0.65 on leakage-safe test sets.

(2) *The apparent successes were artefacts.* The only large and consistent effect, a gain of 1.3 kcal/mol in the raw docking score with rejection sampling in 12 of 15 pockets, was accompanied by worse ligand efficiency in 14 of 15 pockets, molecules that were larger by 12 heavy atoms on average, and a drop of 29 percentage points in PoseBusters validity. The consistent direction of the first, exploratory tier of the physics-anchored route weakened from 89% to 67% when the design was properly sized.

(3) *Guidance had a cost at the strengths at which an effect could be expected.* The physical validity of the molecules decreased dose-dependently with guidance strength (PoseBusters pass rate from 0.56 to 0.17 at λ = 1.0 for the physics-anchored predictor, in all eight pockets), and the molecules fragmented at larger strengths.

(4) *Two measured properties of the predictors and their gradients were consistent with the failures.* The predictor outputs are strongly correlated with ligand size (0.64 to 0.72), and the selection by the ranker is dominated by size. The magnitude of the EGNN gradient was positively correlated with the distance of an atom from the pocket (+0.240; 95% CI 0.194 to 0.285), that is, the strongest pull acted on atoms least likely to form binding contacts. The synthesizability gradient was not explained by three simple proxies. These are correlational findings, and their explanatory role is a hypothesis.

(5) *An apparent generalisation gap of the affinity predictor was largely an artefact of the split.* A validation set that overlapped the training set by 74% of its targets and a test set of 27 pockets suggested that the predictor did not generalise (R² = −0.54). On a target-disjoint split with 127 test targets the same architecture reached R² = 0.34, with a nominal margin over a ligand-only baseline (R² 0.20) that was not tested pairwise with target-level resampling. Similarly, the reported quality of the synthesizability predictor was re-evaluated on a target-disjoint set and was lower (R² 0.41 against 0.77).

(6) *The pre-registered delta-learning experiment was significantly worse than the baselines.* The delta model reached R² = 0.117 against 0.342 for the EGNN and 0.362 for a linear model with two features, it did not beat an absolute-target control on the same backbone, and its best validation error was not below that of a constant predictor. The residual was practically not learnable from structure by this model. The second modification (auxiliary interaction supervision) was designed and its infrastructure verified, but it was not run because the gate was not passed.

@sec Contributions

The contributions of the thesis are the following.

(i) A documented, statistically disciplined mapping of where a widely used class of methods, gradient guidance and rejection sampling with learned predictors, fails for structure-based molecular diffusion, with quality-degradation checks and negative controls applied to every apparent positive result.

(ii) A leakage-safe, target-level split of the labelled CrossDocked2020 subset, and the demonstration that the choice of split and of the resampling unit can reverse conclusions about predictor quality.

(iii) Two mechanistic diagnostics of the guidance gradient that can be reproduced in about twenty minutes, and their results.

(iv) A pre-registered, verified and completed negative test of delta-learning as a target for a guidance predictor, with a reusable, resumable training infrastructure whose isolation of the test set is enforced by an automated test.

(v) An audit trail: numbers in this thesis were re-derived from raw results, and discrepancies found in earlier summaries were corrected.

@sec Limitations

The conclusions are bounded by the design. They concern one generator, one data set with cross-docked poses, guidance on the clean-data estimate with position-only gradients, predictors trained on clean poses, and evaluation by docking, whose proxies for affinity and synthesizability have known limitations. The pocket sets were small (1 to 20), the exploratory tiers had low power, per-pocket effects smaller than about 1 to 1.4 kcal/mol could not be excluded, the seed variance of most guidance predictors was not measured, the powered synthesizability test rests on one pocket, and no experimental validation was possible. Absence of a detected effect is not evidence that no effect exists.

@sec Recommendations for Future Work

The following work follows from the diagnosed failures. First, predictors should be trained on noised inputs at every diffusion time, with an explicit size control or with an objective that aligns the gradient with pocket contacts, and evaluated on the diagnostics of this thesis before any costly sampling study. Second, rankers should be compared within strata of heavy-atom count. Third, the synthesizability experiment should be repeated on multiple pockets and with a rule-based score, to determine whether the divergence is specific to the learned RAscore. Fourth, the auxiliary interaction supervision of Track E, if it is pursued, should be tested first on the absolute target, where the base predictor is known to learn. Fifth, evaluation should include an orthogonal scoring method and, where possible, experimental data, and the pocket sets should be sized by a prior power calculation.

The thesis reports a negative result. It is offered as a result and not as a failure: it locates where a plausible and popular idea does not hold, states the conditions of that statement, and provides the evaluation framework with which the next, better-designed attempt can be judged.
