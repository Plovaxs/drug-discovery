@ch X | CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

@sec Conclusions

The thesis asked whether learned guidance signals improve the molecules of a frozen, pocket-conditioned diffusion model on independently computed evaluation outcomes, that is, on quantities that are computed independently of the guidance model. The question was tested with a frozen TargetDiff generator, position-only guidance on the clean-data estimate, two families of affinity predictors and one synthesizability predictor, and an evaluation harness consisting of AutoDock Vina docking, PoseBusters validity, ligand efficiency and the independently computed RAscore, with corrected statistics, bootstrap intervals and negative controls.

The scope of what follows should be stated before the individual conclusions are: this thesis does not establish that learned guidance fails in general. It establishes that the tested configurations did not produce defensible improvements under the specified generator, data regime, guidance mechanism, predictors, evaluation proxies and computational budget. A different generator, a noise-aware predictor, a gradient that is propagated through the denoiser, or a larger compute budget are all outside what was tested, and Appendix E reports which of these untested directions were in fact pursued afterward and what they changed (none of the eight further checkpoints altered the conclusion below, which is additional evidence for it, not a revision of it). These bounds are drawn explicitly in {fig:scope}. The following conclusions are drawn.

@fig scope | guidance/thesis/figures/fig_scope_boundary.png | 6.3 | The boundary of the null result, drawn from the scope statement above and the limitations of Section X.3. The left panel lists the configuration actually tested, within which no route built on a trained surrogate produced a defensible improvement. The right panel lists what was not tested and to which, therefore, the result does not extend. The figure makes no claim the chapter does not already make in words; it is included because a null result is the kind of finding most often quoted beyond its own evidence, and the two panels are what separate "learned guidance did not work here, under these conditions" from "learned guidance does not work", which is not a conclusion of this thesis.

(1) *No route built on a trained surrogate produced a defensible improvement.* Gradient guidance with an EGNN affinity predictor, with a physics-anchored GIGN + PIGNet2 predictor and with three variants of the mechanism (gradient normalisation, classifier reformulation, timestep windowing), rejection sampling with the EGNN as a ranker, gradient guidance toward synthesizability, and a redesigned training target (delta-learning against the docking score) each failed their pre-declared criteria. None of the 56 pocket-level gradient comparisons of Tracks A and B (9 and 24 in the two tiers of Track A, 23 in Track B) was significant after correction within its checkpoint. The predictors reached Pearson correlations of 0.58 to 0.65 on leakage-safe test sets, and this was originally read as ruling out low predictor accuracy as the explanation. Conclusion (4) below revises this reading: a predictor with no protein input at all reaches a comparable correlation through a class-level prior alone, so correlation magnitude on its own does not establish that these predictors had learned pocket-specific structure–activity relationship.

(2) *The apparent successes of the trained surrogate were artefacts, and a positive control localises the cause to the signal.* The only large and consistent effect obtained with the trained EGNN, a gain of 1.3 kcal/mol in the raw docking score with rejection sampling in 12 of 15 pockets, was accompanied by worse ligand efficiency in 14 of 15 pockets, molecules that were larger by 12 heavy atoms on average, and a drop of 29 percentage points in PoseBusters validity. The consistent direction of the first, exploratory tier of the physics-anchored route weakened from 89% to 67% when the design was properly sized. A pre-registered positive control then replaced the trained surrogate with AutoDock Vina's own zero-shot score in the identical selection mechanism, on the identical 20 pockets, 3 seeds and evaluation protocol: this succeeded, improving the *predicted docking score* (Vina Dock −8.068 against −6.813 kcal/mol, Wilcoxon p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁶) in a way that survived size normalisation (ligand efficiency flat, p = 0.756; PoseBusters pass rate held), while significantly increasing molecular size (+1.8 heavy atoms) and significantly reducing both synthesizability scores. It is therefore a validated harness and a multi-objective trade-off, not a demonstration that better molecules were designed, and the improvement is in a computational score rather than in measured binding.

This is the single most important result of this thesis for interpreting everything that preceded it, and its reach should be stated exactly. It shows that the *selection* harness can detect a change driven by a genuinely informative signal, so an insensitive evaluation design cannot explain the null results of Chapter VI. For the rejection-sampling route it localises the failure directly to the *information content of the trained predictor*, because the mechanism was held fixed while only the signal changed. For the gradient routes of Chapter V it is indirect evidence only: Vina is not differentiable, so the same signal could not be sent through a gradient mechanism, and a mechanism-level contribution to those failures is narrowed but not excluded. The control is also not an independent measurement of binding, since the selecting and evaluating scores are two modes of one scoring function and share its biases (Section VI.5.1).

(3) *Guidance had a cost at the strengths at which an effect could be expected.* The physical validity of the molecules decreased dose-dependently with guidance strength (PoseBusters pass rate from 0.56 to 0.17 at λ = 1.0 for the physics-anchored predictor, in all eight pockets), and the molecules fragmented at larger strengths.

(4) *Measured properties of the predictors and their gradients were consistent with the failures, and a fuller audit identifies what the predictors actually read.* The predictor outputs are strongly correlated with ligand size (0.64 to 0.72), and the selection by the ranker is dominated by size. The magnitude of the EGNN gradient was positively correlated with the distance of an atom from the pocket (+0.240; 95% CI 0.194 to 0.285), that is, gradient magnitude increased, on average, with distance from the atoms most likely to form binding contacts. A later, signed measurement over 23,202 atom-states in 20 pockets found the gradient's *direction* indistinguishable from random (mean cosine toward the nearest contact −0.019, 95% CI [−0.046, +0.008]; 48.5% of atoms pushed toward their nearest contact, which is chance), with its own random-direction and finite-difference controls passing. The two results together say that the gradient is uninformative about the interface, not that it actively opposes it, and earlier phrasing in this project describing a "wrong direction" is withdrawn on that evidence (Section V.4). The synthesizability gradient was not explained by three simple proxies. An eight-block information-content audit (Chapter IV.6) extended this into a systematic comparison: across 40 pairwise, Benjamini–Hochberg-corrected tests, a structure-aware representation significantly beat a ligand-only one in 12 cases, tied in 28, and never lost; a chemically explicit, typed interaction representation (hydrogen bond, hydrophobic, π-stacking and salt-bridge counts) explained almost no variance on its own (R² 0.07–0.08) until a single size covariate was added, at which point it explained four times as much. No representation of the binding pocket tested in this thesis reliably outperformed a model of the ligand alone.

(5) *An apparent generalisation gap of the affinity predictor was largely an artefact of the split, and a fuller audit shows the margin this revealed did not survive a stronger baseline.* A validation set that overlapped the training set by 74% of its targets and a test set of 27 pockets suggested that the predictor did not generalise (R² = −0.54). On a target-disjoint split with 127 test targets the same architecture reached R² = 0.34, with a nominal margin over a weak ligand-only baseline (R² 0.20) that was not tested pairwise with target-level resampling. Against a stronger, 14-descriptor ligand-only model built later in the project (R² = 0.3531), this margin disappeared: the structure-aware predictor was statistically indistinguishable from it (0.3420–0.3437 across three seeds). Similarly, the reported quality of the synthesizability predictor was re-evaluated on a target-disjoint set and was lower (R² 0.41 against 0.77).

(5a) *Most of the predictors' apparent performance is a class-level prior, not structure–activity learning, and this holds for the deployed architecture itself.* A within-target label permutation, which preserves each target's typical affinity range while destroying which ligand carries which label, reproduced 70% of the 14-descriptor model's real-label performance and, when the identical control was run on the deployed EGNN architecture by full retraining, 90% of the EGNN's own real-label performance. The resolvable window beyond this prior, bounded below by the ligand-only ceiling and above by an estimated label-noise ceiling, spans only 0.22 R², narrower than the average width of a single confidence interval measured for this task. Neither activity cliffs nor matched molecular pairs, the two standard diagnostics most sensitive to genuine local structure–activity capture, provide evidence that any predictor in this thesis captures it: sign agreement on the direction of a matched pair's affinity change was at or below chance for both a structure-aware ensemble and a ligand-only model.

(5b) *Augmenting training data with a larger external data set did not close the gap, after a compound-level leak was found and removed before, not after, the production run.* A combined CrossDocked–BindingNet training pool was found to share 0.34% of its rows, but 10.12% of evaluation records by ligand identity, with the validation and test partitions; a filter removing these rows was built and enforced in code before three further training arms were run. A predictor trained on 20,000 CrossDocked rows alone (validation R² = 0.362 averaged over 3 seeds; coincidentally the same figure as, but a different quantity from, the two-feature linear baseline of conclusion (6) below) performed descriptively better than one trained on a matched total of CrossDocked plus leakage-filtered BindingNet rows (R² = 0.272), and the filtered and unfiltered BindingNet-augmented arms performed nearly identically (0.272 against 0.278), indicating the leak identified had not substantially inflated the earlier result even though removing it remained the correct decision in principle.

(6) *The pre-registered delta-learning experiment was significantly worse than the baselines.* The delta model reached R² = 0.117 against 0.342 for the EGNN and 0.362 for a linear model with two features, it did not beat an absolute-target control on the same backbone, and its best validation error was not below that of a constant predictor. The residual was practically not learnable from structure by this model. The second modification (auxiliary interaction supervision) was designed and its infrastructure verified, but it was not run because the gate was not passed.

@sec Contributions

The contributions of the thesis are the following.

(i) A documented, statistically disciplined mapping of where a widely used class of methods, gradient guidance and rejection sampling with learned predictors, fails for structure-based molecular diffusion, with quality-degradation checks and negative controls applied to every apparent positive result.

(ii) A pre-registered positive control, built from a genuine, non-surrogate affinity signal applied through the identical selection mechanism, pockets and evaluation protocol as the thesis's own null results, that validates the evaluation harness directly and localises the repeated guidance failures to the information content of the trained predictors rather than to an insensitive design or to the selection and gradient mechanisms themselves.

(iii) A leakage-safe, target-level split of the labelled CrossDocked2020 subset, and the demonstration that the choice of split and of the resampling unit can reverse conclusions about predictor quality.

(iv) A systematic, eight-block information-content audit of what representation predicts binding affinity on this split, extending a single ligand-only comparison into a corrected, pairwise test across ligand, pocket, raw and size-normalised contact fingerprints and a chemically typed interaction representation, together with a class-prior decomposition, via within-target label permutation, that was run not only for a descriptor pipeline but for the deployed 3-D equivariant architecture itself.

(v) Two mechanistic diagnostics of the guidance gradient that can be reproduced in about twenty minutes, and their results.

(vi) A pre-registered, verified and completed negative test of delta-learning as a target for a guidance predictor, with a reusable, resumable training infrastructure whose isolation of the test set is enforced by an automated test.

(vii) A training-data-composition study (BindingNet augmentation) that found and removed a compound-level leak before, rather than after, its production runs, and that found augmentation did not close the gap to a ligand-only baseline at matched training-set size.

(viii) An audit trail: numbers in this thesis were re-derived from raw results, and discrepancies found in earlier summaries were corrected.

@sec Limitations

The conclusions are bounded by the design. They concern one generator, one data set with cross-docked poses, guidance on the clean-data estimate with position-only gradients, predictors trained on clean poses, and evaluation by docking, whose proxies for affinity and synthesizability have known limitations. The pocket sets were small (1 to 20), the exploratory tiers had low power, per-pocket effects smaller than about 1 to 1.4 kcal/mol could not be excluded, the seed variance of most guidance predictors was not measured, the powered synthesizability test rests on one pocket, and no experimental validation was possible. This thesis is computational throughout: no molecule discussed in it was synthesised, assayed or otherwise tested in a laboratory, and every statement about a molecule "binding better" is a statement about a predicted score. The evaluation criteria used here, predicted docking score, ligand efficiency, physical-pose validity, drug-likeness and retrosynthetic accessibility, are also an incomplete screen even on their own computational terms: they say nothing about absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion or toxicity, nothing about selectivity against off-targets, and nothing about whether a proposed route would succeed in practice. A molecule that passes every check reported in this thesis is a candidate for further computational triage, not a drug-like candidate in the sense a medicinal chemist would use the phrase, and an ADMET and off-target assessment would be a prerequisite for any claim of practical usability. The target-disjoint split used throughout is easier than a leave-superfamily-out split would be, and 40.1% of test-set ligands sit on a scaffold that also occurs somewhere in training, so "held-out target" should not be read as "held-out chemistry" when interpreting any of the predictor-quality results. The experimental labels mix Kd, Ki and IC50 measurements whose composition differs between training and test, a further source of irreducible variance folded into the label-noise ceiling of Chapter IV.8. The positive control of Chapter VI.5 is itself bounded: it tests only a selection-based mechanism, since Vina is not differentiable and a gradient-based analogue with a genuine signal could not be constructed; it therefore directly supports F1 (the trained predictors are uninformative) without directly excluding F2 (a correctly informative signal misapplied by the specific gradient mechanisms of Chapter V) for the gradient-guidance routes considered on their own. Absence of a detected effect is not evidence that no effect exists, and this thesis does not claim equivalence anywhere. The distinction is not rhetorical: a non-significant comparison bounds an effect only as tightly as the design's resolution allows, and no formal equivalence test (two one-sided tests against a pre-declared margin) was run for any route, because no margin of practical indifference was pre-declared. What the design does support is an upper bound from its own power calculation: effects at or above roughly 1.0 to 1.4 kcal/mol per pocket, depending on the sample size of the checkpoint (Section IX.2.4), would have been detected with 80% power, and none was. Effects below that bound are compatible with every null reported here. Readers who need a specific minimum effect of interest, for instance the shift a medicinal chemist would consider worth a sampling run, should compare it against that bound rather than against the p-values.

@sec Recommendations for Future Work

The following work follows from the diagnosed failures. First, predictors should be trained on noised inputs at every diffusion time, with an explicit size control or with an objective that aligns the gradient with pocket contacts, and evaluated on the diagnostics of this thesis before any costly sampling study. Second, rankers should be compared within strata of heavy-atom count. Third, the synthesizability experiment should be repeated on multiple pockets and with a rule-based score, to determine whether the divergence is specific to the learned RAscore. Fourth, the auxiliary interaction supervision of Track E, if it is pursued, should be tested first on the absolute target, where the base predictor is known to learn. Fifth, evaluation should include an orthogonal scoring method and, where possible, experimental data, and the pocket sets should be sized by a prior power calculation. Sixth, and directly motivated by the positive control of Chapter VI.5, a gradient-free particle-guidance mechanism such as SVDD, which requires only an evaluable rather than a differentiable value function, could be paired with a genuine signal such as Vina in place of a trained surrogate, to test whether a more sophisticated mechanism adds value on top of simple reranking once the signal itself is no longer the limiting factor; this was considered and deliberately not run for this thesis, both because of its implementation cost and because the positive control already supplies strong indirect evidence for what such an experiment would show when paired with the trained surrogates examined here.

The thesis reports a negative result. It is offered as a result and not as a failure: it locates where a plausible and popular idea does not hold, states the conditions of that statement, and provides the evaluation framework with which the next, better-designed attempt can be judged.

@sub A proposed extension: uncertainty-aware acquisition (not tested)

The extension described here has not been implemented or tested in this thesis, and no result is claimed for it. It combines a predictor with an uncertainty estimate, an acquisition rule that uses that estimate, and a conflict-aware treatment of the two guidance gradients. Each component is stated with a hypothesis that could fail.

The surrogate would be an affinity predictor with Monte Carlo dropout, giving a mean μ and an uncertainty σ for each complex. Before σ is used for acquisition, its calibration must be shown: σ should correlate with the absolute error on held-out targets of a leakage-safe split. If it does not, an upper-confidence or expected-improvement rule has no basis. This calibration test is the first hypothesis of the extension, and it is to be run before any sampling study.

An upper confidence bound (μ + κσ) or an expected-improvement criterion would enter the reverse trajectory as a correction to the guidance gradient, with a time-dependent weight λ_t that favours exploration early and exploitation late. The weight is written λ_t rather than β_t to avoid a clash with the noise schedule of the diffusion model. The second hypothesis is that the acquisition-guided sampler gives no significant gain on independently computed scores over a random-acquisition control with the same number of steps.

Projecting conflicting gradients (PCGrad) between the affinity and the RA-score gradient would be tested as a remedy for the divergence reported in Chapter VII. The third hypothesis is that the frequency of opposing gradient directions falls without a gain in RA-score.

Any such extension would be judged by the protocol of this thesis: Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, target-clustered bootstrap intervals, and negative controls (random acquisition and shuffled σ). Before any improvement is claimed, ligand efficiency, heavy-atom count and PoseBusters validity must be checked; a gain in docking score alone would not be accepted, for the reasons given in Chapter VI.

A closed-loop simulation, in which docking scores retrain the surrogate and the acquisition function screens generated molecules, would be a simulation only. It would not establish synthesizability or biological activity. Its principal risk is that it optimises the docking score, which Chapter VI showed can be raised by increasing molecular size. The fourth hypothesis is that under such a loop the heavy-atom count rises across iterations while ligand efficiency falls. If the gates above are not passed, the output of the loop is not evidence of design.

These hypotheses are stated so that they can fail, and a null result would be reported in the same way as the results of this thesis.
