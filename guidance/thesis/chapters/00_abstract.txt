@abstract
Structure-based drug design increasingly relies on three-dimensional diffusion models that generate ligands inside a protein pocket. A proposed way of improving such models is guidance with learned predictors of binding affinity and synthesizability. It was tested whether this idea survives when judged by independently computed evaluation outcomes rather than by the predictor's own score. A frozen TargetDiff model was guided by affinity and synthesizability predictors, and results were evaluated with AutoDock Vina docking, PoseBusters validity, ligand efficiency and the real RA-score, using Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, bootstrap intervals and negative controls.

Five routes were investigated: gradient guidance with an EGNN affinity predictor; a physics-anchored GIGN+PIGNet2 predictor and three mechanism variants; non-gradient rejection sampling; guidance toward synthesizability; and a pre-registered residual (delta-learning) training target. A leakage-safe, target-disjoint data split showed that an earlier generalisation gap was largely a split artefact, yet none of the routes produced a defensible improvement. Affinity guidance had no detectable effect across three orders of magnitude of guidance strength, and the predictor's gradient magnitude was positively correlated with the distance of an atom from the pocket (r = +0.24). Rejection sampling improved the raw docking score in 12 of 15 pockets but worsened ligand efficiency in 14 of 15 (on average 12 more heavy atoms, and 29 percentage points lower PoseBusters validity). Synthesizability guidance gave no supported gain; in an exploratory design it raised the predictor's own score while lowering the real RA-score, a divergence that was not statistically confirmed after retraining on a leakage-safe split. The delta-target predictor (test R² = 0.117; target-clustered 95% CI −0.20 to 0.32) was significantly worse than a two-feature linear baseline (R² = 0.362) and than an EGNN (0.34–0.41), because the residual was not learnt better than by a constant.

The thesis contributes a reproducible, leakage-aware evaluation framework, an audited methodology, and a documented negative result that locates where learned guidance signals fail.

Keywords: structure-based drug design; diffusion models; classifier guidance; binding affinity prediction; data leakage; negative results
@endabstract
