@abstract
Structure-based drug design increasingly relies on three-dimensional diffusion models that generate ligands inside a protein pocket. A proposed way of improving such models is guidance with learned predictors of binding affinity and synthesizability. It was tested whether this idea survives when judged by independently computed evaluation outcomes rather than by the predictor's own score, and, where it does not, which of three mechanisms explains the failure: an uninformative guidance signal, a correctly informative signal misapplied through the guidance mechanism, or an objective whose apparent gain can be obtained without a genuine improvement. A frozen TargetDiff model was guided by affinity and synthesizability predictors, and results were evaluated with AutoDock Vina docking, PoseBusters validity, ligand efficiency and the independently computed RA-score, using Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, bootstrap intervals and negative controls.

Five guidance routes were investigated: gradient guidance with an EGNN affinity predictor; a physics-anchored GIGN+PIGNet2 predictor and three mechanism variants; non-gradient rejection sampling; guidance toward synthesizability; and a pre-registered residual (delta-learning) training target. A leakage-safe, target-disjoint data split showed that an earlier generalisation gap was largely a split artefact, yet none of the routes produced a defensible improvement. Affinity guidance had no detectable effect across three orders of magnitude of guidance strength, and the predictor's gradient magnitude was positively correlated with the distance of an atom from the pocket (r = +0.24), while a signed measurement of the gradient's direction over 23,202 atom-states found it indistinguishable from random (mean cosine toward the nearest contact −0.019, 95% CI [−0.046, +0.008]): the signal is uninformative about the interface rather than pointed away from it. Rejection sampling with the trained predictor improved the raw docking score in 12 of 15 pockets but worsened ligand efficiency in 14 of 15 (on average 12 more heavy atoms, and 29 percentage points lower PoseBusters validity). A pre-registered positive control then replaced the trained predictor with AutoDock Vina's own zero-shot score in the identical selection mechanism and pockets: this succeeded, with a significant improvement in predicted docking score (Wilcoxon p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁶) that survived size normalisation (ligand efficiency unchanged, p = 0.756) but came with significantly larger and significantly less synthesizable molecules (both p < 10⁻⁴), so it is a harness validation and a multi-objective trade-off, not a demonstration of successful design. Because the selection score and the evaluation score are two modes of the same docking program rather than independent measurements, the control establishes that the *selection* harness can detect a genuine signal; it localises the failure of surrogate-based *selection* directly to the predictor's information content, and supports the same reading for the gradient routes only indirectly, since a non-differentiable signal cannot be put through a gradient mechanism. An eight-block information-content audit supports this localisation: no representation of the pocket tested, including a chemically typed interaction representation, reliably outperformed a ligand-only model after correction, and a within-target label-permutation control, run for both a descriptor pipeline and the deployed architecture itself, showed that 70 to 90% of apparent predictive performance is a class-level prior rather than within-target structure–activity learning. Synthesizability guidance gave no supported gain; in an exploratory design it raised the predictor's own score while lowering the independently computed RA-score, a divergence that was not statistically confirmed after retraining on a leakage-safe split. The delta-target predictor (test R² = 0.117; target-clustered 95% CI −0.20 to 0.32) was significantly worse than a two-feature linear baseline (R² = 0.362) and than an EGNN (0.34–0.41), because the residual was not learnt better than by a constant, and augmenting training data with a larger external data set (BindingNet), after removing a compound-level leak found before the production run, did not close the gap to a ligand-only baseline at matched training-set size.

The thesis contributes a reproducible, leakage-aware evaluation framework with a validated positive control for its selection harness, a systematic information-content audit of what predicts measured affinity on this split, and a documented negative result that locates where learned guidance signals fail under the tested configurations, with direct evidence that signal quality, rather than the selection mechanism, limits the rejection-sampling route and converging indirect evidence that it also limits the gradient routes.

Keywords: structure-based drug design; diffusion models; classifier guidance; binding affinity prediction; positive control; data leakage; negative results
